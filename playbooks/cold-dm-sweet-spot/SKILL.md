# Cold DM sweet spot

Why this exists: outreach experts report that AI-personalized DMs kill connections: long, hyper-specific hooks about the recipient's work read as machine-written, and the reader can't tell what you want. A plain copy-paste ask beats that. But a bare "can I send you an offer?" from a stranger can look like spam. The sweet spot sits between them.

# Rules
1. Line 1 or 2 states who you are and what you want (the ask). No making them read to the end to find it.
2. Keep ONE short, human reason for writing to them (their raise, a hiring post, a launch). One line, said the way a person would say it.
3. Avoid over-specific stat openers about THEIR content ("your post did 136k, your others got 1k"), quoting their posts back at length, or clever analysis. That's the AI tell.
4. Proof stays light: one stat or one link, plus the portfolio/rates link. No stacked bullet lists in DMs.
5. Aim for 3-6 short lines. Plain words, user's own casing/style rules still apply.
6. Never reference anything you don't fully understand; never claim unverified facts.
7. Don't rewrite every existing draft wholesale; tighten toward this shape.

# Template
```
hey <name>, <who you are in one line>.
<one real reason you're writing>, so <the ask: paid role/retainer>. open to it?
<one proof line or link>
<portfolio/rates link>
```

# Learn from results
- Log each send with the style used (sweet-spot, long-hook, bare ask) in the outreach tracker.
- When replies come in (or don't after the bump window), note which style got them, and adjust the rules here from real results and expert advice, not guesses.
