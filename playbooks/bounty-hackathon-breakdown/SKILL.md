# Bounty & hackathon breakdown

For every bounty or hackathon you surface, open its own page (rules, prizes, judging, eligibility, submission instructions) and produce one card in this exact shape:

**[Name](own page URL)** — Type: one of Writing / Thread, Design, Video, AI / Agentic build, Code, Mixed (name the parts). Add Bounty or Hackathon, and Solo/Team.
- **Deadline:** date and timezone.
- **Short rule:** one or two sentences on what must be submitted, plus any dealbreaker (region lock, no-AI content, required tool, public repo, age, KYC, required on-chain action).
- **Prizes:** the full breakdown as listed, with currency and whether it's cash, token or credits. Note the number of winners and, when known, submissions so far.
- **Post on:** where the work itself must be published, including required tags, hashtags or mentions.
- **Submit on:** where the entry link is handed in, plus any wallet or account needed.
- **Best route to winning:** two or three concrete moves drawn from the judging criteria and past winners, blended with the matching playbook below. If unknown, say so instead of guessing.
- **Effort & fit:** rough hours needed, and a one-line fit for the user's skills.
- **Sponsor check:** Low risk / Medium risk / High risk of scam, with the 2-3 findings behind it (see Sponsor verification). Put this last so it's the final word on the card.

## Sponsor verification (do for every card)
Check quickly, a few lookups max:
- Identity: official site and X account exist, match each other, and the listing links back from the sponsor's own channels.
- Track record: account age and real engagement (not a fresh or botted account); funding, audits or known backers; past bounties on the same platform.
- Payouts: past winners announced and publicly confirm being paid (search the sponsor handle + 'winners' / 'paid'); any complaints about unpaid or rigged results.
- Platform: listed on a vetted board (Superteam Earn, Devpost, DoraHacks) vs a random form or DM.
- Red flags: asks for a fee, seed phrase, wallet connection to unknown sites, KYC beyond the platform's own, buying the token, or vague 'top 25 share a pool' wording with no currency; brand-new token sale promotion; no judging criteria.
Rating: Low = vetted platform + established sponsor + paid winners on record. Medium = some signals missing or unverifiable. High = any hard red flag, payout complaints, or no verifiable identity. Never call something safe without evidence; say what you couldn't check.

## Hackathon winning playbook (patterns from serial winners)
1. Pitch before code: who is hurting, why it matters, what proves you solved it. Judges see ~3 minutes; the build is proof of the pitch.
2. Live demo over slides.
3. 3-minute video: ≤30s problem, 90–120s demo, 30–60s tech woven into the demo.
4. Narrate in your own voice; no AI script or voiceover.
5. Record cleanly (Cap, Screen Studio, OBS with cursor zoom) plus one simple diagram.
6. Hand-written README in short points, no AI jargon.
7. Stack prizes: one project aimed at main prize AND a sponsor 'best use of' prize.
8. Target a specific track or ecosystem rather than the open pool.
9. Keep a running list of events (prize, deadline, online/in-person) and plan entries.
10. Ship often and post every build: one-line hook, link, tag sponsors/organisers; network before the event.
Writers/non-coders: own rules 1–6 for a team, favour storytelling and social-impact tracks.

## Content bounty winning playbook (patterns from serial bounty winners)
1. Volume plus iteration: enter consistently, learn from each loss, come back sharper.
2. Explainer structure: big-picture hook, simple analogy, numbered how-it-works, honest risks, then the product/token tie-in.
3. Open with the reader's real question, not the sponsor's slogan.
4. A visual (screenshot, chart, graphic) or link on every tweet.
5. Name the risks plainly so it reads as analysis, not shilling.
6. Show you actually used the product: explain the mechanics with real screenshots.
7. Follow every format rule exactly (tags, hashtags, IDs, codes, required screenshots) and show the effort.
8. Post early when engagement is scored at the deadline; never buy or pod engagement.
9. After winning, thank the sponsor publicly and stay in their creator/ambassador program; repeat contributors get noticed.
10. Post milestones and reply to every comment; build credibility outside bounties (research, build-in-public).

## Rules
- Never invent prize numbers, criteria, platforms or winner patterns. Mark anything unverified.
- Drop or flag anything the user can't enter (region, closed, invite-only).
- Sort by best reward-to-effort for the user's skills, and group by type when listing several.
- Save long lists to a file and send the top picks in chat.
